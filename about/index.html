A Leak Company Built Around One City's Plumbing

Buena Park is not a generic Southern California suburb, and its leaks are not generic either. This city grew from Rancho Los Coyotes dairy land into the entertainment capital of Orange County in the space of a single lifetime. Walter Knott planted berries here in 1920. The city incorporated in 1953. Within fifteen years, tract housing covered the plain from the Fullerton line to the Cypress border. We built this service around that specific story, because the story decides where the water goes wrong.

Our work is leak detection and leak repair, nothing else. No bathroom remodels, no water softener sales quotas, no drain franchise scripts. When you call (714) 750-8637, you reach a dispatcher who can get a leak specialist to any of our 30 service areas, any hour, every day of the year.

What Working Buena Park Every Week Teaches You

Three facts shape almost every job we run here. First, the water is hard. The City of Buena Park Water Utilities pumps roughly 70 percent of the supply from the Orange County Groundwater Basin, and that groundwater runs 10 to 17 grains per gallon. Hard water scales tanks and fixtures and, more expensively, pits the inside of copper pipe until pinholes open.

Second, the housing stock aged in lockstep. The post-incorporation boom neighborhoods, North Buena Park, South Buena Park, East Buena Park, Los Coyotes, and Crescent among them, share original copper that is now 60 to 70 years old. When we repair a pinhole on one street, the houses around it are usually a few years behind at most. Around Old Town and Downtown, the pre-1953 dairy-era homes add galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains to the picture. The newer pockets near Coyote Hills mix in PEX and PVC.

Third, everything is slab-on-grade. The flat North Orange County plain gave this city no basements, so supply lines run in or under concrete. Finding a leak here rewards patience and instrumentation, not guesswork and jackhammers. That is why our process leads with non-invasive detection methods and treats demolition as a last resort with a defined size.
