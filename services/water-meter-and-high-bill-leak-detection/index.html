Your Meter Cannot Be Fooled

A Buena Park Water Utilities bill that climbs with no visible leak is one of the most common reasons people call us. A slab leak stays under the floor. A silently leaking toilet flapper runs into the bowl and down the drain unnoticed. Your water meter registers every gallon passing through whether or not you can see where that water went, making it the fastest, cheapest diagnostic step before we ever send a truck.

Run the Test Yourself, Right Now

Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, including anything on an automatic cycle like an irrigation timer. Find your meter, note the reading, wait at least two hours without using any water, then read it again. Any movement means water left the system somewhere between the meter and your fixtures, even with everything off. Running water heard with every fixture off is a meter check waiting to happen, and it costs nothing to look at the low-flow indicator before deciding whether to call anyone.

Fast Flow vs. Slow Flow

A fast, steady flow points toward a supply side leak, a slab, yard, or service line failure running continuously. A slower or intermittent flow more often points toward a fixture, most commonly a silently leaking toilet flapper, which can waste hundreds of gallons a day without ever overflowing or making a sound. A bill that has been high for several cycles usually means a smaller, slower leak rather than a sudden failure.

Common Causes Behind a High Bill

Slab leaks are common in Buena Park's boom era tract homes, see our slab leak page. Silent toilet leaks are next most common. Food coloring in the tank showing up in the bowl within ten minutes confirms it. Once you have confirmed loss with the meter test, we combine that flow rate data with acoustic listening and thermal imaging to pinpoint exactly where the water is going.

High Water Bill? Water Meter Leak Detection Questions

My bill has been high for months, not just one cycle. Does that matter?

It usually points to a smaller, slower leak rather than a sudden failure, since a large leak tends to show up dramatically within one cycle. Slow leaks are exactly what the meter test is built to find.

Could this just be a meter reading error?

It happens, but rarely. If your own meter test confirms flow with everything off, that rules out a reading error and confirms real water loss somewhere in your system.

How much water does a running toilet actually waste?

A worn flapper can waste 200 gallons a day or more, silently, enough on its own to visibly move a monthly bill.
